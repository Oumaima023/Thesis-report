\chapter*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Introduction}

The increasing automation of logistics operations is transforming the way industrial transport
systems are designed and operated. \glspl{av} are becoming an integral part
of modern logistics yards, where they perform repetitive transport tasks with the objective
of improving efficiency, safety, and operational continuity. Unlike conventional
\glspl{agv}, autonomous yard vehicles
operate in dynamic, semi-structured environments shared with human workers, manually driven
vehicles, and subject to changing environmental conditions. These characteristics introduce new
challenges for monitoring and evaluating operational performance.

\gls{oee} is a widely adopted methodology for assessing industrial
performance. By combining the three dimensions of Availability, Performance, and Quality,
\gls{oee} provides a concise measure of how effectively equipment converts operating time
into productive work. However, its original formulation was developed for stationary
manufacturing equipment and does not adequately reflect the characteristics of autonomous logistics
operations, where mission variability, remote supervision, and \gls{odd} constraints
significantly influence system behaviour.

This work was carried out at TII KAMAG within an industrial project focused on autonomous
yard logistics. Its objective is to design and implement an \gls{oee}-based performance
measurement framework adapted to autonomous vehicle operations. The proposed framework
preserves the fundamental principles of classical \gls{oee} while extending them to account
for the specific characteristics of autonomous yard logistics through an adapted time hierarchy,
autonomous-vehicle-specific loss classification, \gls{odd}-aware time accounting, and
complementary engineering indicators supporting the evaluation of reliability, operational
capacity, supervision requirements, execution quality, and environmental influences.

This work is organised as follows. The first chapter presents the industrial context, the
autonomous transport system, the available operational data, and the relevant state of the art.
The second chapter introduces the proposed \gls{oee} framework, including its measurement
principles, time hierarchy, indicators, and canonical data model. The third chapter describes
its implementation and evaluates the framework using industrial data and controlled synthetic
datasets, including a mission-phase diagnostic extension. This work concludes by summarising
the main findings, limitations, and perspectives for future deployment.
